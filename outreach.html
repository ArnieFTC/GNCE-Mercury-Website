Third-year outreach

Francis Bitter Magnet Laboratory at MIT

Oct 9, 2026MIT, Cambridge

We spent an afternoon at MIT’s Francis Bitter Magnet Laboratory, now part of the Plasma Science and Fusion Center. Dr. Jagadeesh Moodera, a senior research scientist in MIT Physics, hosted us. His group studies thin films of magnetic and superconducting materials.

He showed us copper plates from the lab’s Bitter magnets and walked us through the vacuum systems and cryostats his group uses. We finished at a lab bench, cooling a superconductor with liquid nitrogen and testing it with small magnets.

Thank you to Dr. Moodera for hosting us.

Touring VulcanForms

Oct 31, 2026Metal 3D printing

We toured VulcanForms, a Massachusetts company that makes metal parts with 3D printing. It prints them on laser powder bed fusion machines it designs and builds itself, where dozens of lasers melt fine metal powder one thin layer at a time. The parts are then machined and inspected in-house before they go to customers in aerospace, medical, energy, and other industries.

Tours like this show us how engineers work outside of FTC. Thank you to VulcanForms for having us.

Mentoring Phuthanang Youth Trust

OngoingOnline, South Africa

Phuthanang Youth Trust (PYT) is a community organisation in Soweto, South Africa. Since 2004 it has helped young people in Jabavu with skills training and education. We meet with their students over video calls to help them get started in FIRST Tech Challenge.

On our first call we explained how an FTC season works: a new game every year, a robot you design, build, and program yourself, and matches played in alliances with other teams. We also talked about what robotics has taught us, from strategy and technical skills to leading a team.

We’ll keep meeting with them to answer questions and share what our first 2 seasons taught us.

Robotics and engineering summer camp

Week of Aug 17, 2026

We spent a week of the summer running a robotics and engineering camp for about 13 students in grades 4–⁠8. Campers learned the basics of both VEX IQ and FIRST LEGO League: building drivetrains and mechanisms, writing block code, and running missions on a real FLL table.

Between robot sessions, campers took on hands-on engineering challenges, like an egg drop and designing and flying their own airplane gliders.

On Friday, the last day, campers showed off their robots to their families at a showcase, driving VEX robots on the field and running their FLL missions. The camp inspired many VEX teams to start.

Second-year outreach

Coaching Weston’s FLL teams

Sep 23, 20252025–⁠26 FLL season

We helped Weston’s FIRST LEGO League grow from 4 teams to 17 teams and 122 students, including 10 brand-new 4th and 5th grade teams at Field School. Money raised from our summer camp covered registration for all 10 of those teams, and the camp helped recruit and train new parent coaches.

On Sep 23 we ran a practice scrimmage so the teams could get a feel for a real qualifier. All season we mentored coaches and students on running weekly meetings, programming their robots, and managing their time. 9 of the 10 Field School teams competed at MA qualifiers, and 2 advanced to States.

“The FLL season for the elementary teams would not have been a success if not for the support and guidance from the high school FTC 26413 team.”

Jessie Chan, lead coach, Field School FLL

“It was so well organized. The teams learned a lot today.”

FLL coach, after the scrimmage
