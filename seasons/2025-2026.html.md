## The robot

### Winning alliance, Sustain Award & a trip to States

Early on we considered a sorting indexer so we could score patterns for the ranking point. It would have been more complex, less reliable, and slower, so we built a direct-transfer robot instead: a parallel-plate chassis, a full-width gecko-wheel intake, a 0.34-second transfer, and a flywheel shooter on a 360° turret that tracks the goal with a Limelight.

## Parts of the robot

Follow a ball through the robot: choose a part to see what it does.

![CAD render of the DECODE robot, with each part marked](https://mercury26413.org/images/decode-robot-cad.webp)

1. **Intake** · A compliant bar of gecko wheels that runs the full 14‑inch width of the chassis. Vectored wheels push the balls to the center.
2. **Transfer** · Custom rails on a constant incline get a ball to the shooter in 0.34 s. Our first transfer took 1.5 s.
3. **Shooter** · Version 4 of our flywheel shooter has stable side plates with the motors in front. A lookup table of 7 calibrated distances (1.1–3.34 m) sets its speed, from 3000 to 3975 RPM.
4. **Turret** · A 1:3 herringbone gear on a lazy susan turns the shooter through a full 360° to track the goal.
5. **Limelight** · The Limelight camera sits right in front of the shooter. A PD controller uses it to hold the turret within ±1° of the target.
6. **Chassis** · Parallel plates (version 3) that keep parts easy to remove for maintenance and let us play defense.

## Mechanisms & iterations

### Chassis

![CAD render of the V2 parallel-plate chassis](https://mercury26413.org/images/decode-chassis-v2-ink2.jpg)

V1’s parallel plates gave us maneuverability and defense but had no mounting holes, and the U-channels we added made it heavy. V2 added holes and cut weight, but its wood plates could break. V3 keeps components easy to remove for maintenance.

### Intake

![Mercury’s V2 intake with a horizontal bar of gecko wheels](https://mercury26413.org/images/decode-intake.jpg)

Our first intake, 2 gecko wheels on 2 motors, needed too much precision. V2 used 1 motor and a horizontal bar of gecko wheels. V3 is compliant, runs the full 14‑inch width of the chassis, and uses vectored wheels to push balls to the center.

### Transfer

![CAD render of the V3 transfer with custom rails](https://mercury26413.org/images/decode-transfer.webp)

V1 took 1.5 s to bring a ball to the shooter. V2 moved several balls at once in 0.9 s. V3 uses custom CADed rails on a constant incline to shrink the footprint and gets a ball to the shooter in 0.34 s.

- V1 · **1.5 s**
- V2 · **0.9 s**
- V3 · **0.34 s**

### Shooter

![CAD render of shooter version 4](https://mercury26413.org/images/decode-shooter.jpg)

4 versions: a bulky fixed hood, a sturdier but large build, a smaller modular one that was unstable, and V4 with stable side plates, the motors in front, and a front-mounted Limelight.

### Turret

![CAD of the turret top plate, lazy susan, and herringbone gear](https://mercury26413.org/images/decode-turret.webp)

A pulley drive lost tension and took up space, so we switched to a 1:3 herringbone gear mounted to a lazy susan in the top plate. Spring-loaded key retractors manage the wires through the full 360°.

### Limelight

![CAD render of the Limelight mounted on the front of the shooter](https://mercury26413.org/images/decode-limelight.webp)

After trial and error, we mounted the Limelight directly in front of the shooter. It gives more accurate shots and also helps support the shooter’s structure.

## Software & autonomous

### Turret tracking: search, track, lock, hold

A PD controller turns the turret toward the target and holds within ±1°. If the Limelight drops a frame, an 8-frame buffer keeps it from snapping into search mode, and it sweeps back toward where it last saw the target, cutting re-acquisition time by 50%. Encoder-based limits stop the turret before it can damage the gearbox.

### Shooter: distance-based RPM

A lookup table maps 7 calibrated distances (1.1–3.34 m) to flywheel speeds from 3000 to 3975 RPM, with linear interpolation in between and a PIDF loop holding the speed. Inside 1.3 m, the hood angle changes for short shots.

### Autonomous: 12 balls

We use Pedro Pathing, and thanks to other teams we ran a newer, unreleased version that made our autonomous much more consistent. The close-side routine shoots the preload, then collects and scores triples 3 more times for 12 balls.

### Strategy: defense with a parallel plate

Our code is organized into subsystems, and we tune our turret, flywheel, and autonomous loops live with telemetry. In matches, the parallel-plate chassis lets us play defense and block the other alliance from scoring.
